Research date: 6 September 2026 (Europe/London). Publication cutoff: **4 September 2026**, unchanged.

## Finding

The corpus still has meaningful omissions beyond the 26 existing records awaiting expanded evidence review. This bounded pass identifies **33 newly itemised document/version candidates**, alongside **20 previously discovered candidates still outside the corpus**: **53 main-queue candidates**, not 53 approved additions. ## What the omissions tell us

The current collection is stronger on the AI Act legislative/implementation pathway than on adjacent institutional AI-policy production. The missing material clusters around expert-group recommendations, Parliament resolutions outside the AI Act procedure, agency governance/supervisory documents, research funding/infrastructure, and independently citable historical programme instruments. This is an evidence-based diagnosis of these discovered gaps, not a measured coverage percentage for each institution.

The highest-priority admission review is for the substantive texts: the HLEG recommendations, Parliament's 2020 AI resolutions, EDPB Opinion 28/2024, the Digital Europe and EuroHPC AI provisions, and established sector guidance/roadmaps. Historical programme texts should remain a clearly labelled lineage, not be presented as legal predecessors of the AI Act. Database expansion does not automatically enlarge the RP's analytical sample.

## Remaining verification and boundary checks

- Obtain inaccessible English PDFs or reliable equivalent official manifestations. Do not promote official summaries or search extraction to full-text verification.
- Keep the HMA/EMA 2023 workplan's exact adoption day unresolved: a scheduled meeting date is not proof of actual adoption on that day. The handoff was corrected to null during consolidation.
- Preserve ENISA's month-only issue date, EDPB's separate adoption/publication dates, Parliament's adoption/OJ dates, and Europol's later website-update ambiguity.
- Map provisional classification labels to the approved vocabulary only after evidence review. In particular, expert-group authorship differs from Commission authorship, and two EMA committees do not automatically establish joint institutional provenance.
- Assess the EIOPA factsheet's substantive centrality and document-type eligibility before admission. Its official existence and useful content do not settle the inclusion decision.
- Resolve independently citable version/attachment identities. Do not count press releases, portal pages, alternate-language URLs or print/PDF formats as extra documents.

## Search coverage and limits

The pass used official-domain title/identifier searches, institution publication pages, primary EUR-Lex texts/PDFs, and backward/forward links from official strategy pages and known reports. Exact queries and access failures are retained in each handoff. Local duplicate screening used the 131 canonical records, 171 inventory entries and preceding 34-candidate discovery handoff; it was not an exhaustive search of Git history or every other working note.

No institution's entire catalogue was paginated to exhaustion. Further follow-ups remain in historical ESPRIT/FP series, EDPS generative-AI guidance and older opinions, ENISA 2020 material, the Parliament criminal-AI resolution, EU-OSHA/Frontex series, JRC and AI-in-science publications, HLEG definition versions, court judgments, EESC/CoR output and other sector-specific documents. Some official sites returned robot checks, 403/429 responses, oversized-document errors or inaccessible downloads. Inaccessible is not absent.

The result supports **“additional omissions identified”**, not **“all EU AI documents collected”**. Existing publication gates and the 4 September cutoff remain unchanged.
